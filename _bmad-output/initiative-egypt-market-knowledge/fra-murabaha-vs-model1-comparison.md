# FRA Sharia Murabaha model vs conventional Model (1)

Prepared 2026-10-03 for the scholar file. It compares FRA's guiding **"consumer-finance agreement on the Murabaha and service-Murabaha basis"** with FRA's conventional **Model (1)**, the single-transaction consumer-finance contract (2026 rulebook pp. 91–96, unchanged since Decree 869/2021; see [the 2021/2026 comparison](fra-model-contract-comparison-2021-2026.md)). It records what each document says. **It makes no Sharia finding.** Every question below is for the scholar.

**Status of the Murabaha model.** FRA's page states that the Central Sharia Supervisory Committee has **certified (اعتماد) Murabaha, Musharaka and Ijarah products and their associated contracts** for consumer finance, SME/micro and real-estate finance. The template itself does not state whether it is one of the certified contracts, or whether it is binding as a minimum the way Model (1) is. It is titled "guiding" (استرشادي).

**Aman wording in the template.** The party fields are blank, but the preamble asks the reader to read the agreement "before using **Aman** financing services". The Murabaha definition says "**Aman** buys the goods from suppliers… the price is paid in instalments **to Aman**". With Aman #43 on FRA's Islamic-Murabaha register, the template appears derived from Aman's product. It remains a lead for Aman's actual contract (AMAN-01), not proof of it.

## Bottom line

1. **Different shape.** Model (1) is a one-off loan-style contract. The Murabaha model is a one-year, auto-renewing **framework agreement** under which each purchase from an approved merchant becomes a separate Murabaha sale. In shape it resembles the conventional card **Model (2)** more than Model (1).
2. **Different legal story, same mechanics in places.** Model (1) says the company *finances the customer's purchase* at a *return rate*. The Murabaha model says the company *buys and resells* at *cost plus a known profit*, yet still describes its subject as "providing the financing needed to purchase". It also has the supplier **invoice the customer directly**.
3. **The Murabaha model fills the gaps Model (1) leaves.** It covers late payment (a grace period, then a "damage" amount directed to charity), acceleration, defects and returns, a priority claim on insolvency or death, and how the contract is formed (purchase, then OTP). Model (1) is silent on all of these.
4. **Several provisions are for the scholar.** These are the order and evidence of ownership and possession, the binding promise to buy, the "actual damage" late payment and its expenses, the early-settlement rebate left to company policy, and the assignment of the debt portfolio.

## Topic-by-topic comparison

| Topic | Conventional Model (1) | Murabaha model | What differs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | One contract per financing; term = repayment period (Cl. 5) | Framework agreement for a renewable "financial year" (Cl. 5); each purchase is a Murabaha under it | Framework vs single deal |
| **Subject** | Company "provides the financing needed to purchase" goods/services (Cl. 3) | Same phrase, "according to the principles of Islamic Sharia" (Cl. 2). The definitions describe Murabaha as the company **buying from suppliers and reselling**, and service-Murabaha as the company **buying the service and authorizing the provider** to deliver it | Sale framing added in the definitions; the financing wording is kept |
| **Who the company is** | Licensed consumer-finance company | Same; defined as financing "instalment purchases **through third parties**" (من خلال الغير) | |
| **Price** | Amount financed, financing costs, **annual/monthly return**, admin and other fees, total payable (Cl. 4 table) | Credit limit and a **"profit rate …% annually"** (Cl. 4-2). Per purchase, a statement of **cost of goods, sale price**, instalments and due dates (Cl. 4-4). Service-Murabaha price = purchase cost + known profit (% or lump sum) | Return on financing vs cost + profit; both expressed as an annual rate |
| **Ownership and delivery** | Not addressed; customer buys from the seller. The bar on disposal (Cl. 8) implies customer ownership | Company **authorizes the supplier/merchant to deliver to the customer and issue the invoice in the customer's name directly** (Cl. 4-1). Customer acknowledges having inspected and **received** the goods and being "authorized to receive" them (Cl. 7-3) | **Key point for the scholar** |
| **Promise and formation** | Signature of the contract | Customer **undertakes to buy** what the company buys, at cost plus profit (Cl. 4-3). Any purchase from an approved merchant is **acceptance of the company's offer**, which concludes the Murabaha (preamble). Customer undertakes to accept the company's request to sell "the goods the customer **bought or requested**" (Cl. 4-5). Entering the OTP (written "OTB") counts as acceptance **and as acknowledgement of receipt** (Cl. 4-6) | Sequence of offer, acceptance, purchase and receipt |
| **Merchant network** | None | Company maintains a list of approved merchants on its site (Cl. 3) | |
| **Early settlement** | From the 2nd instalment, 30 days' notice; **commission of …% on the remaining balance** (Cl. 10) | Same timing and notice; balance per **the company's internal early-settlement schedule** (Cl. 6). No commission named, no rebate stated | Commission vs company policy |
| **Late payment** | **Absent** | **7-day grace period**. From day 8, without notice, the customer pays the **"actual damage"** on the late instalment, calculated monthly, **minimum EGP 20**, plus **all company expenses** until payment; "this damage penalty is **directed to charity after deducting expenses**" (Cl. 8-2) | Present only in the Murabaha model |
| **Acceleration** | Absent | After **two** missed instalments the company may demand the whole debt "**if the customer's solvency is established**" (Cl. 8-2) | |
| **Collection fee** | Absent | Fixed fee per instalment if collected at home (Cl. 8-3) | |
| **Priority claim** | Absent | Full value of the goods is a **privileged debt** on insolvency or death (Cl. 8-4) | |
| **Defects and returns** | Absent | Customer may exchange or return defective goods **to the merchant**, under the merchant's return policy and warranties (Cl. 8-5) | The company, as seller, refers defects to the merchant |
| **Security** | Register a security interest over the goods (Law 115/2015), cost borne by the party named; no disposal until fully paid (Cl. 8) | Same register, cost borne by **the company**; no sale of the goods without reverting to the company and paying the balance (Cl. 11-1, 11-2) | Cost bearer; wording of the bar |
| **Notes and cheques** | Promissory notes / bills / cheques as "guarantees", returned per instalment (Cl. 4) | "Sufficient guarantees" for instalments; final clearance on full payment, after which any signed guarantees are void (Cl. 11-3) | |
| **Debt transfer** | Company may **securitize or discount** its debt portfolio without customer objection (Cl. 11) | Company may **assign (إحالة)** its debt-portfolio rights "**by methods consistent with Sharia**", without customer objection (Cl. 12) | "Discount" dropped; Sharia condition added |
| **Data and contact** | Confidentiality; disclosure to FRA and credit bureaus (Cl. 12) | Broader: disclosure to iScore, FRA and other regulators; authorizes the company to contact the customer's **employer, residence, banks and government bodies** to verify and to **collect**, with no breach-of-confidence claim (Cl. 9) | Wider consent |
| **Amendment** | Written agreement of both parties (Cl. 7) | **No amendment clause** | |
| **Admin fees** | Customer bears the listed admin fees (Cl. 9) | No admin-fee clause; only the home-collection fee | |
| **Disputes and costs** | Economic court, or arbitration by separate agreement (Cl. 14) | Economic court at the company's head office; Civil Code and Law 115/2015 added; **customer bears court costs and lawyers' fees if they lose** (Cl. 13) | Cost-shifting added |
| **Sharia governance** | — | Says "without contradicting Sharia" and "permissible to finance under Sharia", but **names no Sharia committee and no standard** | |
| **Insurance** | Absent (2026 has a separate insurance Model (3), Board Decree 28/2026) | Absent | Neither |

## Questions for the scholar

These are flagged by Mushir and not decided by it. The standards named are candidates from the project's AAOIFI catalogue for the scholar to select or reject.

1. **Ownership and possession before resale.** Under Cl. 4-1 the supplier delivers to the customer and invoices the customer by name. Under Cl. 7-3 and 4-6, the OTP and the customer's acknowledgement stand in for receipt. Does the company acquire ownership and real or constructive possession before it sells to the customer, and does the customer act as the company's agent in receiving? *(SS 8 Murabahah; SS 18 Possession)*
2. **Binding promise to buy** (Cl. 4-3, 4-5), and formation by a purchase at the merchant followed by an OTP. *(SS 49 Promise; SS 8)*
3. **Late payment.** A charity-directed amount described as "actual damage", calculated monthly with a minimum, after deducting the company's expenses, plus all collection expenses. *(SS 3 Procrastinating Debtor)*
4. **Acceleration** conditioned on established solvency; **priority claim** on insolvency or death.
5. **Early settlement** left to an internal company schedule. Is any rebate given, and how is it set? *(SS 8)*
6. **Assigning the debt portfolio** "by Sharia-consistent methods". Does the assignment change the debt's character, to whom may it go, and at what price? *(SS 7 Hawalah; the catalogue has no sale-of-debt standard)*
7. **Guarantees** (notes and cheques, security interest), and the **cost-shifting** of litigation fees. *(SS 5 Guarantees)*
8. **Defects** referred to the merchant, while the company is the seller in the Murabaha.
9. **The "financing" wording kept in the subject clause** alongside a sale definition: does it matter for characterization?

## What this means for the pilot

- **Aman (AMAN-01).** FRA lists Aman Consumer Finance #43 for Islamic Murabaha, and the template carries Aman's name. Request Aman's **current certified Murabaha agreement and schedule**, and ask whether it matches this template.
- **B.TECH / Mylo (BTECH-01).** FRA lists B.TECH Consumer Finance #48 for Islamic Murabaha. Mylo's "Sharia-compliant" marketing is therefore backed by an FRA product licence (the link between Mylo and #48 is otherwise still `verified`, not `established`). Request the Mylo Murabaha agreement.
- **ADI / Takka.** Licensed for Islamic Murabaha, but FRA says its contract is **still before the Central Sharia Supervisory Committee**.
- **Everyone else.** No other consumer-finance licensee is on FRA's Islamic-product list. "Sharia-compliant" claims by any other consumer-finance company have no FRA product licence behind them, as of the page dates above.
