Summary

Where Mushir stands

Mushir does not state that something is permissible or prohibited unless a scholar-approved rule covers it. No rule is approved yet, so it currently answers every such question with "scholar review needed".

Our test set checks that behaviour as a feature. The draft verdicts written by the development team are kept as targets for you to confirm.

What it does not mean

  • Mushir gave no wrong verdict. In all 15 cases it declines and queues the question for you.
  • Saying "I don't know" here is not a failure. It is the correct V1.6 behaviour, and it is reported next to how many questions Mushir can answer, so refusing everything never looks like success.
  • We changed no draft verdicts ourselves.

What we need

  • Appoint a reviewing scholar. Nothing in Mushir has been scholar-reviewed so far.
  • The scholar chooses an option (A–D) for each of the 15 cases.
  • The scholar reviews the five draft rule cards, starting with late-payment charges.
  • You confirm four default settings.

Fastest path once a scholar is appointed: start with the two cases that matter most for Egyptian instalment plans, GC-005 (late-payment charges) and GC-017 (rescheduling with a new profit margin). Both already have a draft rule card in this pack (late-payment charges and rescheduling), so the scholar reads and signs rather than writes.

How Mushir decides

Every question passes the same checks in order. Mushir moves to the next check only when the current one is satisfied. Where a check fails, Mushir stops and asks a question or names the missing document.

Why a rule card instead of the AI's own knowledge? The AI can recite well-known rulings, but it cannot show who approved them, under which school or standard, and with which conditions. A rule card records all three, is approved once by the scholar, and then applies to every question it covers. If the scholar later changes a rule, every future answer changes with it.

What the tests check

Our test set measures Mushir's general features: understanding Arabic, Egyptian Arabic and English, showing a short reasoning summary with every answer, citing its sources, asking one clear question when a fact is missing, and saying "I don't know" correctly. The ruling questions were first written expecting a direct verdict. Here is the same question as first written and as tested now.

As first written · draft verdict

GC-017: Is Murabaha rollover with a new profit margin on the outstanding balance permissible?

Expected: PROHIBITED, citing AAOIFI SS-08.

A verdict drawn from retrieved text, with no record of scholar approval.

Now · what Mushir says and the test checks

GC-017: the same question.

"This scenario requires explicit rule evidence and human review before Mushir can provide a safe non-binding assessment."

"تتطلب هذه المعاملة مراجعة قواعد شرعية وأدلة مصدرية إضافية قبل تقديم تقييم آمن؛ يرجى إحالتها إلى مراجع شرعي مؤهل."

The test passes only if Mushir stops at the approved-rule check, shows the AAOIFI sources it found, answers in the user's language and places the case in the scholar review queue. Declining for any other reason counts as a failure.

Once the scholar approves a rule card for a case, the same test switches to checking the scholar-confirmed verdict, and Mushir answers citing the approved rule and its sources. Each approval widens what Mushir can answer; later training material from you widens it further.

The 15 cases

Thirteen cases are ruling questions: the draft answer is a verdict. Two are routing questions: the draft answer is a contract type that the question's wording does not establish. Three ruling questions (GC-004, GC-011, GC-018) also exposed a fault in how Mushir recognised some Arabic phrasings. That is now fixed: they reach the approved-rule check and your review queue like the others.

For each case we show the draft expected answer as written in the test file, what Mushir says now, and what the scholar should confirm. The draft answers are not a ruling. They were written by the development team in May 2026 from AAOIFI standards, with no scholar review. The label "Sharia Scholar" in the test files names the authority each answer needs, not someone who reviewed it. See question 5 below.

Five draft rule cards for Egyptian instalment plans

A rule card turns one Sharia rule into a checklist: the facts that decide it, what each combination of facts means, and the one question Mushir asks when a fact is missing. We drafted five cards for the questions Egyptian buyers ask most. None is approved and none is used by Mushir. The outcomes are placeholders for the scholar to confirm, change or reject.

Each card names its AAOIFI standard. Before signing, we confirm the exact paragraph against the indexed standards text with you. Each card also waits on one open question: which source wins when your own rule files and AAOIFI differ.

The iPhone question
StandardsSS-08, SS-19
Deciding factsWho finances; whether the financier owns the item before selling it

Decision options

For each case, the scholar picks one option. A short note is welcome but not required.

A · Approve as written

The draft answer is correct as stated. We turn it into a rule card for the scholar's signature, and Mushir answers it citing that card.

B · Approve with conditions

The answer depends on a fact, for example who gives a guarantee. Mushir asks that one question, then answers according to the card.

C · Keep deferring

Not ready to decide. Mushir keeps saying "scholar review needed", which is already what the test expects in V1.6. Nothing changes until you decide.

D · Change the expected answer

The draft is wrong or incomplete. The scholar states the correct answer or behaviour, and we update the test and the card.

Questions you may have

1. Does "I don't know" mean Mushir is failing?

No. In V1.6, declining a ruling question that no approved rule covers is the correct behaviour, and our tests check that Mushir does it properly. They also report how many questions Mushir does answer, so a system that refused everything could not pass. Each rule you approve turns a set of these questions into answered ones.

2. Did Mushir give any wrong Sharia answer?

No. In every one of the 15 cases Mushir declined and referred the question for scholar review. It never answered "permissible" where the draft says "prohibited", or the reverse.

3. Did you change the draft verdicts to make the tests pass?

No. Every draft verdict is kept exactly as written, as the target for you to confirm. What changed is what the test checks before a rule is approved: correct, sourced, queued abstention instead of a verdict. Changing a verdict without the scholar would mean a developer deciding a Sharia matter.

4. These are well-known rulings. Why can't Mushir just answer them?

Mushir could repeat them, but that is the risk we want to avoid. A ruling that is "well known" may still carry conditions (see GC-003, GC-007 and GC-010), follow one school over another (GC-019), or differ from your own rule files. A rule card makes those choices visible and approved.

The cost is small: each approval is one decision that then covers every future question on that topic.

5. Who wrote the expected answers in the first place?

The development team wrote them in May 2026, working from AAOIFI standards, so that Mushir could be tested from the start. No scholar has reviewed them. Each one is labelled "Sharia Scholar" to mark the authority it needs, not one it already has. This pack is therefore the first scholar review of these answers, which is exactly why Mushir must not present them as rulings yet.

6. We don't have a scholar yet. What happens until we do?

Mushir stays safe and useful without one. It answers definitions and explains what AAOIFI standards say, with sources. For any question that asks whether something is permissible, it says scholar review is needed and queues the question.

What waits for the scholar: every rule card, these 15 decisions, and the review of about 100 cases that the V1.6 release depends on. The sooner a scholar is appointed, the sooner Mushir can give its first approved conclusions.

When choosing a scholar, it helps if they can review Arabic and English, know AAOIFI Shari'ah Standards, and are familiar with Egyptian consumer finance.

7. How much of the scholar's time will this take?

We expect one working session. Each case is a choice of A–D plus, for some, a one-line answer. We prepare every rule card in advance, so the scholar only reads and signs. Six cases have a condition question and may need a little more thought.

8. What happens if the scholar disagrees with AAOIFI or with our own rule files?

Mushir follows the approved rule card, never a blend. This is the open decision from our last update: which source wins when your rules and AAOIFI differ, or whether every difference goes to the scholar. We recommend sending every difference to the scholar until a pattern is clear.

9. What does a user see today if they ask one of these questions?

A short message saying the question needs rule evidence and scholar review before Mushir can give a safe, non-binding assessment, in the user's language. The question is placed in the scholar review queue automatically, so real user demand shows which rules to approve next.

10. After approval, will Mushir say "this is haram"?

No. It will say what the approved rule finds, cite the rule and the scholar's approval, and remind the user it is not a fatwa. For example: "Under rule [name], approved by our reviewing scholar on [date], adding a new profit margin to the remaining Murabaha balance raises a riba issue."

11. Why are GC-005 and GC-017 the priority?

V1.6 focuses on Egyptian instalment plans. Late-payment charges and rescheduling appear in almost every consumer-finance contract, including Contact's published terms. The other thirteen cases cover Sukuk, Mudaraba, Musharaka, Wakalah, Istisna, currency exchange and guarantees. They matter for completeness but come up less often in instalment questions.

12. Why did you remove the confidence percentage?

A percentage looks precise, but ours had never been checked against scholar decisions, so a "92%" could not be trusted. Each answer now shows which sources it used and how old each source is. We will show a certainty measure only after it has been tested against scholar-reviewed answers, as agreed in the strategy.

13. What is stored about users, and for how long?

Each answer is saved with the question, the facts the user stated and the decision checks, so the scholar can review it. Each record carries a random session code, not an account or name, though a user may still type personal details into a question. Nothing is deleted while the scholar review is under way. After it ends, records older than 365 days are deleted only once that policy is switched on as a recorded decision; your input is the period in the settings table.

14. What if the scholar chooses option C for everything?

That is a safe outcome. Mushir keeps deferring these questions, exactly as it does today. The prototype stays less useful for those topics until rules are approved, but it never guesses.

15. When will the V1.6 prototype be ready to review?

The deal-question lane and its safety checks are built, seven pilot companies have identity records from FRA's registers and their own pages, and request letters to the first five are drafted. The remaining inputs are listed under Open questions and requests: a reviewing scholar, the scholar's decisions in this pack (at least GC-005 and GC-017), the first approved rule cards, confirmation of the pilot companies, and approval to send the provider letters. With those, we publish V1.6 on Hugging Face for review. V1.6 is ready to move on when about 100 fixed test cases pass the feature checks and the scholar's review of them finds no wrong conclusions. Your feedback then shapes the next release.

Test questionA fixed question with an expected result, run automatically on every change to make sure nothing gets worse.
Critical ruling questionsThe 19 test questions where a wrong answer would be serious, such as calling a prohibited arrangement permissible.
